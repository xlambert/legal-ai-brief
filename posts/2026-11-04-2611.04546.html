Can an AI Assistant Pass the EU AI Act Exam?

A new benchmark gives regulatory assistants a test, and most of them did not beat the free baseline

Paper: Are LLM Assistants Regulatory Ready? Answers from regenold's 2026 EU AI Act Q&A Challenge (arXiv 2611.04546)

Every few weeks someone sends me a vendor deck promising an assistant that knows the EU AI Act cold. The slides are always lovely. What I almost never see is a test score, so I was glad to find a paper that finally gives these tools an exam.

Marco Virgolin, Thomas Haszprunar and Maximilian Regenold built the EU AI Act Q&A Benchmark: 110 open-ended questions about Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, each with an exemplary answer, a set of correctness criteria, and the references a good answer should cite. They ran a public challenge on it in 2026. Three off-the-shelf baselines went up against five purpose-built assistants, four from commercial developers and one from an academic team.

What happened

Only two of the five specialist assistants consistently beat the baselines. One of them scored well across every measure. The other three did no better than a general model that anyone with a subscription could use.

The authors say it plainly: specialist development alone does not guarantee better performance. I would put that sentence on a slide at the next vendor demo.

How they graded it

The grading is what makes this useful to me. Answers were scored on correctness, reference quality, conciseness, tone and latency. They also ran a multi-turn hard mode where a simulated user pushes back on a correct answer. Anyone who has watched a lawyer lean on a chatbot until it folds knows why that matters.

Reference quality deserves its own line. A correct-sounding answer that cites the wrong article is a problem I have seen before, and it is the kind of problem that gets past a tired reviewer at 6 p.m.

What I would do with this at a law firm

If your firm advises clients on AI Act compliance, or builds its own assistant to help the team, you now have a public yardstick. The benchmark code is on GitHub. Run it against whatever you are piloting, and run it against the base model too. If the expensive tool and the base model score the same, you have learned something about the price.

It also changes the procurement conversation. I would ask every regulatory-assistant vendor whether they have taken this benchmark, what they scored, and how they did on the pushback questions. A vendor who has not heard of it is telling you something.

There is a limit to what 110 questions can tell us. The benchmark covers one regulation, and the law will keep moving through guidance and delegated acts. A score today is a snapshot. I would treat it as a floor for the questions you ask, and keep your own lawyers' hard questions on top.

The training angle

This works as a teaching tool too. Give associates twenty of the questions, have them answer cold, then compare against the assistants and the exemplary answers. They will learn the Act and they will learn how much to trust the tool in the same hour.

I will say what I think. Regulatory assistants are going to end up in every compliance group, and the firms that test them will be ahead of the firms that trust the brochure. A public exam is a good start.

So here is my question. If a vendor handed you their score on a test like this one, would you know what a passing grade looks like for your clients?
