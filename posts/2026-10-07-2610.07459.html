Who Writes the Record?

A new arXiv paper gives us a better way to check the claims vendors make about their AI agents

If you've filled out or read an AI vendor security questionnaire in the last year, you've seen sentences like these. "A human approves every external communication." "All agent actions are logged." "The system has been evaluated and is safe for deployment."

I've always read those lines and thought, okay... says who? And how would I know?

A new paper on arXiv takes that exact question seriously, and I think it gives law firm buyers a pretty useful tool.

The idea in one sentence

In Auditable Claims about AI Agents, Yue Zhao and colleagues argue that a claim about an AI agent can only be checked if four things are nailed down first: the policy the claim is about, the scope it covers, the records that would settle it, and who writes those records. They borrow this from the preconditions auditors use before an assurance engagement. If those pieces aren't fixed before anyone looks, you can't really audit the claim. You can only take someone's word for it.

They add three conditions that are specific to agents. There needs to be an independent record of what the agent did, the authorization has to be tied to the specific arguments of each action (the approval should point to the specific email, recipient, and content that went out), and you need proof the log is complete. A log can be perfectly tamper-proof and still be missing half the entries.

They also anchor this in the EU AI Act. Article 12 requires high-risk systems to support automatic logging of events, but it doesn't tell you which records prove which claims. The paper tries to close that gap, and includes a claim-check table that walks six common claims through current NIST, IETF, and OWASP drafts.

How I'd use this at a firm

Take the human approval claim. A vendor tells you an attorney approves every outbound action the agent takes. Using this framework, the follow-up questions write themselves. Which actions does this cover? What record shows the approval happened? Is that record written by the agent itself or by something independent? Does the approval cover the actual content that went out, or just a general go-ahead?

Those are good questions for procurement, and they're even better questions for our own internal deployments. When we tell clients that a lawyer reviews everything the AI produces, can we show the record? Who writes it? Could we produce it if a client's outside counsel guidelines or an auditor asked for it?

Clients with EU operations are going to start asking us exactly this. Some already are, at least informally. I'd rather have the answer ready than scramble for it.

A caution

This is a position paper with a formal model behind it. It doesn't tell you how to build the logging. Some of the proofs depend on assumptions that won't always hold in a messy real-world deployment. That's fine. The value here is the checklist mindset, and the claim-check table is something I'd hand straight to whoever runs our AI vendor reviews.

The paper closes with practical steps for operators, buyers, auditors, and standard setters, which tells you the authors expect this to be used. I'm planning to take them up on it.

So the next time a vendor tells you a lawyer approves every action their agent takes, what's the one record you'd ask to see before you believed it?
